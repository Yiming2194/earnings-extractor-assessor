Virtuix Reports Second Quarter Fiscal 2027 Results, Including 105% Omni One Revenue Growth and 3.3X Order Increase

 

Total Quarterly Revenue Rises 55% Sequentially to $1.2 Million Following Meta Quest Launch

 

Enterprise Orders Increase 45% as Virtuix Expands Robotics, Defense, and Healthcare Applications

 

Company Enters into Non-Binding Letter of Intent to Acquire STS International as Part of Strategy to Expand Presence in Defense Market

 

AUSTIN, TEXAS – November 16, 2026 – Virtuix Holdings Inc. (NASDAQ: VTIX) (the “Company”), a leading developer of full-body simulation systems, today reported financial and operational results for the second quarter of fiscal year 2027 ended September 30, 2026.

 

Key Second Quarter Fiscal 2027 Results and Subsequent Highlights

 

●Revenue Growth: Omni One revenue increased 105% to $1.1 million, while total revenue increased 21% year-over-year and 55% sequentially to $1.2 million. Omni One revenue, representing 93% of total revenue, was partially offset by a $0.3 million decline in legacy Omni Arena revenue.

 

●Omni One Momentum: The number of Omni One unit orders increased 3.3X year-over-year following the June 2026 launch of Omni One for Quest in collaboration with Meta. Virtuix has since become an official Meta reseller, launched bundles with Meta Quest 3 and Quest 3S headsets, and expanded its library to more than 75 compatible or upcoming Quest titles.

 

●Enterprise Adoption: Omni One Enterprise orders increased 45% year-over-year, including repeat orders from Tesla for its Optimus humanoid robot program and a first order from Figure AI, following prior sales to NASA, KBR, Sirica Therapeutics, and research institutions.

 

●Proposed Acquisition of STS International: Signed a non-binding letter of intent (“LOI”) to acquire all of the outstanding equity interests of STS International Inc. (“STS”), an Arlington, Virginia-based defense technology and services company, for base consideration of approximately $12 million, plus potential earnout payments and other additional consideration. STS has advised the Company that it generated approximately $40 million in unaudited, cash-basis revenue for the 10 months ended October 31, 2026.

 

●Expanding Government Customer Base: Sold Omni One systems to the U.S. Army’s Training Innovation Facility at Fort Leavenworth and the U.S. Navy’s Morale, Welfare and Recreation Liberty Program.

 

●Marine Corps Programs: Advanced the four-person Infantry Fireteam Trainer as lead systems integrator with KBR and AVRT, and showcased its counter-drone training platform, developed with LeadTech, at the Maneuver Warfighter Conference.

 

 

 

 

●U.S. Air Force SBIR: Following its selection in May 2026 for Phase I funding under the U.S. Air Force’s AFWERX Small Business Innovation Research (“SBIR”) program to advance development of the Virtual Terrain Walk (“VTW”) platform, Virtuix is preparing to submit a proposal for Phase II funding.

 

●Healthcare Expansion: Sold its first Omni One systems to the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs for the Valor Center at the Detroit VA Medical Center. The LP Foundation also joined Sirica Therapeutics in Virtuix’s autism therapy initiative, with a multi-site pilot scheduled to begin in early 2027.

 

●Intellectual Property: Secured its 27th U.S. patent, covering proprietary footwear technology, with five additional patents pending.

 

Management Commentary

 

“Our second quarter demonstrates that Virtuix’s commercial strategy is gaining traction. Omni One revenue more than doubled, unit orders increased 3.3 times, and Enterprise orders grew 45%, reflecting increasing adoption across consumer and professional markets,” said Jan Goetgeluk, Founder, Chairman and CEO of Virtuix.

 

“At the same time, we are advancing our strategy to build a scaled defense technology and services business. Our proposed acquisition of STS International represents an important potential step in that direction, combining Virtuix’s proprietary simulation technology with an established defense contractor’s operating capabilities, contract vehicles, and customer relationships that could accelerate sales opportunities for our Omni training systems across the U.S. military and federal government.

 

“Our priorities are clear: convert growing demand into revenue, expand higher-margin Enterprise opportunities, pursue larger defense programs, complete the proposed acquisition subject to its conditions, and reduce operating costs as we drive toward profitability,” concluded Mr. Goetgeluk.

 

“As we continue to scale Omni One revenues and expand into new markets, we remain focused on managing expenses and improving operating efficiency,” said Thomas McGinnis, Chief Financial Officer of Virtuix. “With much of the infrastructure needed to operate as a public company now in place, we see opportunities to streamline our cost structure, specifically in the areas of accounting, investor relations, and operational overhead. We expect cash burn to decline as revenue grows and we implement these cost-saving measures.”

 

Second Quarter Financial Results

 

Net sales for the three months ended September 30, 2026 increased 21% year-over-year to $1.2 million from $1.0 million and 55% sequentially from $0.8 million. Omni One sales increased 105% to $1.1 million from $0.5 million, primarily driven by the launch of Omni One for Quest in collaboration with Meta Platforms, Inc., partially offset by a $0.3 million decline in legacy Omni Arena sales.

 

Gross profit increased 30% sequentially to $0.3 million, compared to $0.4 million in the prior-year period. Gross margin was 25%, compared to 41% a year ago, reflecting a shift in sales mix toward Omni One from higher-margin Omni Arena products and services.

 

Total operating expenses were $5.1 million, compared to $1.9 million in the prior-year period, primarily reflecting an increase in non-cash stock-based compensation, increased marketing investments to build the 2026 Holiday sales funnel, and higher general and administrative expenses associated with operating as a public company, including professional services and insurance costs.

Total other expense, net, was $1.5 million, compared to $0.3 million in the prior-year period, primarily reflecting non-cash debt-related amortization and warrant modification expenses, partially offset by non-cash gains from changes in the fair value of financial instruments.

 

Net loss was $(6.4) million, or $(0.19) per share, compared to $(1.9) million, or $(0.09) per share, in the prior-year period, and narrowed from $(7.2) million in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. The increase was driven primarily by higher non-cash financing expenses as well as higher professional services expenses related to operating as a publicly traded company.

 

Adjusted EBITDA loss, a non-GAAP financial measure, was $(3.6) million, compared to $(1.2) million in the prior-year period.

 

Cash and cash equivalents totaled $5.0 million as of September 30, 2026, compared to $9.5 million as of March 31, 2026. The Company also received $2.6 million in gross proceeds from warrant exercises during the six months ended September 30, 2026.

 

As previously disclosed, prior-period amounts have been revised to correct immaterial errors related to the accounting for certain financing arrangements.

 

Proposed Acquisition of STS International and Launch of Virtuix Defense

 

As previously announced on November 12, 2026, Virtuix has entered into a non-binding LOI to acquire STS International. Under the terms contemplated by the non-binding LOI, Virtuix would acquire all of the outstanding equity interests of STS International for base consideration of approximately $12 million, consisting of approximately $8 million in cash and $4 million in shares of Virtuix Class A common stock, on a debt-free, cash-free basis, subject to negotiation and execution of definitive agreements and satisfaction of closing conditions. The LOI also contemplates potential contingent earnout payments, additional shares issuable under stock-price protection provisions, and possible additional consideration for certain intellectual property assets. Under the binding provisions of the LOI, Virtuix agreed to pay STS International a $100,000 exclusivity fee, which is nonrefundable but would be credited against the cash consideration if the transaction closes. STS International President David Morgan is expected to join Virtuix to lead Virtuix Defense upon closing of the acquisition, and to be appointed to Virtuix’s Board of Directors, subject to customary conditions.

 

STS International, headquartered near the Pentagon, provides defense technology and services spanning C5ISR, engineering, sustainment, and training and simulation. STS International has advised Virtuix that, driven in part by its participation in the C5ISR Gateway to Sustainment contract vehicle, STS achieved strong year-over-year revenue growth in 2026. Based on unaudited cash-basis financial information provided by STS, STS has advised Virtuix that it generated approximately $6.7 million in revenue in 2025 and approximately $40 million in the first 10 months of 2026, with approximately $0.6 million in cash-basis net income during that period. Virtuix has not completed its due diligence, and these amounts may differ materially from audited financial statements prepared in accordance with GAAP.

 

STS International has advised Virtuix that it holds a GSA Multiple Award Schedule, SeaPort-NxG, and other Department of Defense indefinite delivery, indefinite quantity (“IDIQ”) contracts, and that it maintains CMMC Level 2 cybersecurity compliance and a facility security clearance supporting classified work. Virtuix believes these contracts and capabilities could accelerate sales of Virtual Terrain Walk, Infantry Fireteam Trainer, and other Omni-based training systems across the U.S. military and federal government.

The proposed transaction remains subject to, among other things, satisfactory completion of due diligence, an audit of STS International’s financial statements, Virtuix obtaining financing for the cash portion of the consideration, required governmental, contractual, corporate, stockholder, and third-party consents, receipt by Virtuix’s Board of Directors of a fairness opinion, the absence of a material adverse change, execution of definitive agreements, and other customary closing conditions.

 

The parties currently intend to complete due diligence, execute definitive agreements, and close the proposed transaction during Virtuix’s fiscal year ending March 31, 2027, although there can be no assurance as to timing. The LOI is non-binding with respect to the proposed acquisition, and there can be no assurance that the parties will enter into definitive agreements or that the proposed transaction will be completed on the terms described above, within the anticipated timeframe, or at all.

Note About Non-GAAP Financial Measures

 

EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA are non-GAAP financial measures. The Company defines EBITDA as net loss adjusted to exclude provision for (benefit from) income taxes, interest expense, net, and depreciation and amortization. The Company defines Adjusted EBITDA as net loss adjusted to exclude: (i) provision for (benefit from) income taxes, (ii) interest expense, net, (iii) depreciation and amortization, (iv) stock-based compensation expense, (v) financing expense, (vi) gains or losses on extinguishment of debt, and (vii) gains or losses from changes in the fair value of financial instruments. EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA are not calculated in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP”) and should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for net loss or any other measure of performance calculated in accordance with GAAP. The Company believes EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA provide useful supplemental information to investors regarding the performance of its ongoing operations, and management uses these measures to evaluate operating performance and allocate resources. Because EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA are not determined in accordance with GAAP and are susceptible to varying calculations, they may not be comparable to similarly titled measures presented by other companies. About Virtuix

 

Virtuix Holdings Inc. (NASDAQ: VTIX) is a leading developer of immersive, full-body immersive simulation systems and the creator of the “Omni” omni-directional treadmill. Virtuix’s technology lets users physically move through virtual and AI-generated environments. The Company’s products are deployed across consumer entertainment, defense training and simulation, robotics, healthcare, research, and enterprise applications involving organizations including Meta, NASA, Tesla, KBR, and Sirica Therapeutics. The Company’s growing federal and defense footprint includes programs, deployments, and research initiatives involving the U.S. Army, Marine Corps, Navy, and Air Force.

 

Virtuix continues to expand the Omni brand from immersive entertainment into a broader, full-body simulation technology platform serving multiple commercial and government markets. For more information, visit virtuix.com or the Company’s new Investor Relations website at invest.virtuix.com.
