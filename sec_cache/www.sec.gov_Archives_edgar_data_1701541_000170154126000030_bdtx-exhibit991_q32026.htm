Black Diamond Therapeutics Announces Positive Updated Silevertinib Phase 2 Results, FDA Feedback Supporting a Clear Path to Potential Registration in 1L EGFR-NCM NSCLC, and Provides Corporate Update
•Updated Phase 2 results demonstrate robust CNS activity, including 86% CNS-ORR and mPFS double that of currently available EGFR TKIs, in patients with untreated brain metastases
•Recent End-of-Phase 2 meeting with the FDA supports pivotal development in all 1L EGFR-NCM NSCLC patients with potential for accelerated approval based on CNS-ORR
•Cash, cash equivalents, and investments of $102.0 million as of September 30, 2026, is expected to be sufficient to fund operations into the second half of 2028
•Company to host an investor call today at 7:30am ET and will also participate in a fireside chat at the Stifel 2026 Healthcare Conference at 3:20pm ET
CAMBRIDGE, MA, November 10, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) – Black Diamond Therapeutics, Inc. (Nasdaq: BDTX), a clinical-stage oncology company developing MasterKey therapies that target families of oncogenic mutations in patients with cancer, including silevertinib, a potential best-in-class brain-penetrant epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) inhibitor, today reported updated data from its Phase 2 trial of silevertinib in frontline non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients with EGFR non-classical mutations (NCMs), the outcome from its End of Phase 2 meeting with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and associated Phase 3 plans, and financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.
“There remains significant unmet medical need in NSCLC patients with non-classical EGFR mutations, particularly in patients with brain metastases ,” said Alex Spira M.D., Ph.D., Director of the Thoracic and Phase I Program at Virginia Cancer Specialists and Chief Scientific Officer of NEXT Oncology, and clinical investigator on the silevertinib Phase 2 trial. “Along with the exciting CNS response rate and progression free survival data from this Phase 2 trial, it is remarkable that not a single frontline patient treated with silevertinib has developed de novo brain metastases.”
“Brain is the most common site of distant metastasis for patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC”, added Sergey Yurasov, M.D., Ph.D., Chief Medical Officer of Black Diamond Therapeutics. “We believe that silevertinib’s ability to significantly delay CNS progression is the key driver of the durable clinical benefit we are seeing in patients both with and without baseline brain metastases and is the foundation of the pivotal program design in 1L NSCLC.”
“Our recent end of Phase 2 meeting with the FDA was highly collaborative, and we believe the feedback we received supports a clear path to potential accelerated and full approval for silevertinib in frontline NSCLC patients with EGFR non-classical mutations,” said Mark Velleca, M.D., Ph.D., President and Chief Executive Officer of Black Diamond Therapeutics. “This trial design offers a differentiated path to potential accelerated approval in EGFR-NCM patients, who represent about 25% of the frontline EGFR mutant patient population.”
Silevertinib Recent Developments and Upcoming Milestones:
•Silevertinib 1L NSCLC Phase 2 Results Update: As of an October 7, 2026 data cutoff date, with a median patient follow-up of 15.9 months, updated results from the Phase 2 trial (conducted in the U.S. and Canada) of silevertinib dosed at 200 mg once daily (QD) in 43 frontline NSCLC patients harboring a broad spectrum of EGFR NCMs, were as follows:
◦Intention to Treat (ITT) Population: n=43


▪60% ORR (RECIST 1.1), 93% DCR, 15.0 month mPFS (95% confidence interval (CI): 11.0, 23.5)
▪18 patients remained on therapy, with the longest at 26.2 months
▪No new safety signals were observed
◦Patients with untreated brain metastases: n=16 (7 evaluable by RANO-BM)
▪86% CNS-ORR (RANO-BM)
▪12.2 months systemic mPFS (95% CI: 8.1, not estimable (NE)) by RECIST 1.1
◦Patients without brain metastases: n= 24
▪23.5 months mPFS (95% CI:11.0, NE)
▪No patients with progression in the brain
•Cohort 4 (n=20) of the Phase 2 trial is enrolling frontline patients with non-classical EGFRm NSCLC at the planned pivotal dose of 150 mg administered once daily; the first patient was dosed in the third quarter of 2026
•Summary from End of Phase 2 FDA Meeting on October 1, 2026:
◦Based on the FDA’s feedback, the Company intends to pursue a Phase 3 development program for silevertinib that will encompass all treatment-naïve patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC whose tumors harbor an oncogenic EGFR mutation other than a classical mutation (ex19del, L858R) or exon 20 insertion.
◦The FDA also provided feedback on a potential accelerated approval based on CNS-ORR per RANO-BM by blinded independent central review (BICR) and a potential full approval based on PFS per RECIST v1.1 by BICR.
◦The FDA indicated that systemic ORR and overall survival (OS) will need to be evaluated to show systemic ORR similarity to, and no evidence of an OS detriment compared to, the control arm.
◦The FDA acknowledged the Company’s justification for 150 mg QD as the pivotal dose. Safety data from a sufficient number of patients treated at 150 mg QD in the ongoing Cohort 4 are required to be submitted to the FDA prior to opening the Phase 3 trial.
•Phase 3 Trial Plan: The Company is planning a global Phase 3 registrational trial of silevertinib in frontline NSCLC patients with EGFR-NCMs with the potential to initiate in the second quarter of 2027. This trial would enroll 300 patients with EGFR-NCMs and untreated brain metastases randomized 1:1 to receive silevertinib 150 mg once daily or physician’s choice of osimertinib or afatinib. The primary endpoints are CNS-ORR (RANO-BM by BICR) for potential accelerated approval and systemic PFS (RECIST 1.1 by BICR) for potential full approval. The Phase 3 trial would also include an additional 80 patient single-arm cohort in frontline NSCLC patients with EGFR-NCMs without brain metastases, and a 20 patient single arm cohort in patients with leptomeningeal disease. Assuming a second quarter of 2027 initiation, preliminary data anticipated in 2028.
•Phase 2 Trial in Newly Diagnosed GBM: The randomized portion of the Phase 2 trial of silevertinib in patients with newly diagnosed EGFRvIII+ glioblastoma (GBM) is enrolling patients at 150 mg QD, with the first patient dosed in the fourth quarter of 2026. The primary endpoint is PFS (RANO-BM by BICR), with OS as a secondary endpoint. The 150 mg QD dose of silevertinib for the randomized portion of the study was selected based on 6 patients dosed at 150 mg QD in combination with temozolomide (TMZ) in the completed safety run-in portion of the trial, in which no dose-limiting toxicities were observed. Interim PFS data are expected in the first half of 2028 (NCT07326566).

About Silevertinib
Silevertinib is an investigational oral, covalent, brain-penetrant fourth-generation tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) that selectively targets classical and more than 50 non-classical EGFR mutations in NSCLC. It is also designed to potently inhibit key EGFR alterations seen in GBM, including EGFRvIII, while avoiding the paradoxical EGFR activation reported with reversible TKIs. To date, over 200 patients with EGFRm NSCLC or EGFR-altered GBM have been treated with silevertinib.
In addition to the ongoing Phase 2 trial of silevertinib in patients with non-classical EGFRm NSCLC, the Company is also conducting a randomized Phase 2 trial of silevertinib in patients with newly diagnosed EGFRvIII-positive GBM (NCT07326566).
About Black Diamond Therapeutics
Black Diamond Therapeutics is a clinical-stage oncology company developing MasterKey therapies that target families of oncogenic mutations in patients with cancer. The Company’s MasterKey therapies are designed to address a broad spectrum of genetically defined tumors, overcome resistance, minimize wild-type mediated toxicities, and be brain penetrant to treat central nervous system disease. The Company is advancing silevertinib, an investigational brain-penetrant fourth-generation EGFR MasterKey inhibitor targeting EGFR-mutant NSCLC and GBM.
