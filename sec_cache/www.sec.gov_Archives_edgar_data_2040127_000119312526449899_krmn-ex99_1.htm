Karman Space & Defense Reports Third Quarter Fiscal Year 2026 Financial Results

HUNTINGTON BEACH, Calif., November 5, 2026 – Karman Space & Defense (NYSE: KRMN), today reported third quarter fiscal year 2026 financial results.

Third Quarter Fiscal Year 2026 and Subsequent Highlights

 

•
Record quarterly revenue of $202 million, driven by 25% organic growth and 66% total growth year over year
•
Record quarterly net income of $21 million, an increase of 174% year over year, and earnings per fully diluted share of $0.16, compared to $0.06 in the prior-year quarter
•
Record quarterly adjusted EBITDA of $61 million, up 60% year over year, and adjusted earnings per fully diluted share of $0.19, compared to $0.10 in the prior-year quarter
•
Net cash provided by operating activities was $27 million and free cash flow was $13 million
•
Backlog reached a record $1.7 billion at quarter-end, increasing 108% compared to fiscal year-end 2025
•
Generated quarterly bookings of nearly $465 million across all end markets, including a significant seven-year contract with Lockheed Martin supporting the THAAD program
•
Completed the acquisition of Walker Precision Holdings Limited, expanding Karman’s presence in the European defense market
•
Increased fiscal 2026 outlook to $755 million to $760 million in revenue and $225 million to $227 million in adjusted EBITDA

 

“We delivered another quarter of record results, highlighted by 66% total revenue growth, 25% organic revenue growth, 60% adjusted EBITDA growth, $13 million of free cash flow, and a doubling of backlog compared to fiscal year-end 2025,” said Jon Rambeau, Chief Executive Officer of Karman Space & Defense. “With significant visibility into our increased fiscal 2026 guidance, we are focused on building backlog for 2027 and beyond while supporting our long-term organic revenue growth algorithm of 20% to 25%.”

“The award of our second long-term agreement in the quarter further extends our planning horizon and enables us to allocate capital and resources more efficiently. We are currently negotiating two additional long-term agreements, executing the initial phases of two second-source opportunities, and actively pursuing three more. In this strong demand environment, we continue to go on offense, gaining market share while creating value for shareholders.”

“We are excited about the opportunities ahead as we expand our team, broaden our global footprint, and continue to strengthen our organization. Our unique merchant supplier position enables us to support the highest-priority defense programs that enhance national security, while our Space and Launch business provides critical products supporting both space exploration and emerging commercial space opportunities. Across our portfolio, we see substantial opportunity for continued growth. We remain focused on executing our strategy and delivering long-term shareholder value.”

We supply capabilities to more than 150 customers across more than 150 programs, and across the full program lifecycle. The increase in total revenue by end market for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026 as from the comparable periods in the prior year was described as follows.

Growth in Hypersonics and Strategic Missile Defense revenue for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026 from the comparable periods in the prior year was primarily driven by an increase in the production of key interceptor programs associated with Munitions Acceleration Council priorities, and increased production for a new surface-to-surface missile system.

A decline in Space and Launch revenue for the three months ended September 30, 2026 from the comparable period in the prior year was primarily driven by customer order timing associated with shifting launch schedules, partially offset by increased content from liquid-fueled space and launch programs. Growth in Space and Launch revenue for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 from the comparable period in the prior year was primarily driven by the timing of orders from emerging space launch and legacy providers associated with shifting launch schedules.

Growth in Tactical Missiles and Integrated Defense Systems revenue for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026 from the comparable periods in the prior year was primarily driven by an increase in core production programs, including unmanned aircraft systems and counter-UAS, and emerging programs transitioning to production, partially offset by a quarterly decline in a high-volume program.

Maritime Defense Systems revenue for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026 was primarily driven by legacy and next-generation submarine and surface vessel programs.

Backlog

As of September 30, 2026, total backlog was $1.7 billion, which represents the total value or current estimated value of existing contracts, less amounts previously invoiced. Contract types include, but are not limited to, purchase orders, long-term agreements and contractual authorizations to proceed.

Business Outlook for the Full Year 2026

For the full fiscal year 2026, Karman raises its expectations for total revenue to between $755 million and $760 million, and for adjusted EBITDA to between $225 million and $227 million, excluding the impact of any future acquisitions.

Karman has not provided a quantitative reconciliation of adjusted EBITDA on a forward-looking basis to the most directly comparable financial measure calculated and presented in accordance with GAAP because such a reconciliation is not available without unreasonable efforts due to the high variability, complexity and low visibility with respect to charges excluded from adjusted EBITDA, particularly event-driven transactional and other non-core operating items. The magnitude of these items, however, may be significant.

Non-GAAP Supplemental Information

We present in this press release certain financial information based on our EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA margin, adjusted earnings per fully diluted share (adjusted EPS) and free cash flow. EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA margin, adjusted EPS and free cash flow are not measures calculated in accordance with GAAP, and they should not be considered an alternative to any financial measures that are calculated under GAAP. We believe these non-GAAP financial measures, each of which is discussed in greater detail below, are important supplemental measures because they exclude unusual or non-recurring items as well as non-cash items that are unrelated to or may not be indicative of our underlying business performance. Further, when read in conjunction with our GAAP results, these non-GAAP financial measures provide a baseline for analyzing trends in our underlying businesses and can be used by management as a tool to help make financial, operational and planning decisions. We may use non-GAAP financial measures in certain management compensation plans, debt covenants, internal budgetary decision-making, and other resource allocation decisions. These measures are often used by analysts and other interested parties to evaluate companies in our industry by providing more comparable measures that are less affected by factors such as capital structure.

We recognize that these non-GAAP financial measures have limitations, including that they may be calculated differently by other companies or may be used under different circumstances or for different purposes, thereby affecting their comparability from company to company. In order to compensate for these and the other limitations discussed below, management does not, and readers should not, consider these measures in isolation from or as alternatives to the comparable financial measures determined in accordance with GAAP. Readers should review the reconciliations below and should not rely on any single financial measure to evaluate our business.

We define these non-GAAP financial measures as follows:

EBITDA refers to net income before income taxes, depreciation and amortization and interest expense.

Adjusted EBITDA refers to EBITDA plus, as applicable for each period, adjustments for certain items management believes are not indicative of the underlying business performance. Adjusted EBITDA excludes non-cash stock-based compensation expenses. Additionally, adjusted EBITDA excludes certain nonrecurring costs that management excludes in contemplation of budget decisions and are not costs of operating the business, such as entity-wide rebranding initiatives or acquisition integration costs, and lender and administrative agent fees associated with discrete amendments. Lastly, adjusted EBITDA excludes other non-recurring costs including gains or losses from disposition of assets, non-cash impairment losses, non-recurring transaction expenses and other charges or gains that Karman believes are not part of the ongoing operations of its business. The resulting expense or benefit from these other non-recurring costs is inconsistent in amount and frequency.

Adjusted EBITDA margin is calculated by dividing Adjusted EBITDA by revenue.

EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA margin are driven by changes in volume, performance, contract mix and general and administrative expenses and investment levels. Performance, as used in this definition, refers to changes in profitability and is primarily based on adjustments to estimates at completion on individual contracts. These adjustments result from increases or decreases to the estimated value of the contract, the estimated costs to complete the contract, or both.

Adjusted EPS represents GAAP net income (loss) per fully diluted share, excluding transaction-related expenses, integration expenses and non-recurring restructuring costs, lender and administrative agent fees, stock-based compensation and other charges or gains that we believe are not part of the ongoing operations of our business, as they are not representative of our business performance.

Free Cash Flow represents net cash provided by (used in) operating activities in the period less payments for property and equipment made in the period.

Free Cash Flow is considered a non-GAAP financial measure under the SEC’s rules. Management believes, however, that Free Cash Flow is an important financial measure for use in evaluating the Company’s financial performance, as it measures our ability to generate additional cash from our business operations. Free Cash Flow should be considered in addition to, rather than as a substitute for, net income as a measure of our performance or net cash provided by (used in) operating activities as a measure of our liquidity. Additionally, our definition of Free Cash Flow is limited and does not represent residual cash flows available for discretionary expenditures due to the fact that the measure does not deduct the payments required for debt service and other obligations or payments made for business acquisitions. Therefore, we believe it is important to view Free Cash Flow as supplemental to our entire statement of cash flows.
