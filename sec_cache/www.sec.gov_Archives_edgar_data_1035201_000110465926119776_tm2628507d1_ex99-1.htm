California Water Service Group Reports Solid Third Quarter 2026 Results and

Successful Execution of Business Strategy

 

SAN JOSE, Calif., October 28, 2026 -- California Water Service Group (Group or Company, NYSE: CWT), a leading publicly traded water utility serving California, Hawaii, New Mexico, Washington, and Texas, today reported solid financial results for the third quarter 2026.

 

Financial Highlights

 

·Recorded net income of $65.4 million and diluted EPS of $1.05 in Q3 2026, compared to net income of $61.2 million and diluted EPS of $1.03 reported in Q3 2025.
·Invested $148.3 million in water system infrastructure.
·Received approval from the Washington Utilities and Transportation Commission (WUTC) on the Washington Water Service (Washington Water) General Rate Case (GRC) that will add $4.1 million to annual revenues.
·Received change-of-control approval from the public utilities commissions in Nevada and Oregon for water and wastewater system acquisitions.
·Declared the 327th consecutive quarterly dividend in the amount of $0.335 per common share.

 

According to Chairman and Chief Executive Officer Martin A. Kropelnicki, the Company’s solid financial performance reflects the continued successful execution of its business strategy, including improving water system sustainability and keeping rates affordable.

 

“We are making investments needed to provide safe, reliable water service; working with our regulators to recover costs while keeping rates affordable; focusing on maintaining a strong balance sheet; preparing for emergencies that can affect our customers, including extreme weather events and wildfires; and making a difference in our communities. We delivered strong results by maintaining a laser focus on executing our strategy and putting our customers first,” he said.

 

Q3 2026 Financial Results

 

·Q3 2026 revenue increased $21.0 million or 6.7% to $332.2 million, compared to revenue of $311.2 million in Q3 2025.
◦Rate changes and new customers added $22.1 million in revenue, while changes in the Drought Response Memorandum Account (DREMA) contributed an additional $3.9 million.

 

 

 

 

◦Decline in customer water usage offset revenue increases by $4.4 million.

 

·Q3 2026 operating expenses increased $8.9 million or 3.7% to $249.5 million compared to Q3 2025 operating expenses of $240.6 million.
◦Water production costs were consistent between the quarters with higher wholesale water costs, partially offset by lower customer usage.
◦Other operations expenses increased $4.1 million compared to Q3 2025 primarily due to a $2.8 million increase in water conservation program expenditures.
◦Income tax expense on operating income increased $7.1 million compared to Q3 2025 due to higher pre-tax income and a scheduled reduction in the Tax Cut and Jobs Act (TCJA) refund.

 

·Net other income decreased $6.0 million during the quarter due to a $3.9 million reduction in non-qualified benefit plan investment gains and an increase of $1.4 million in business acquisition expenses.

 

YTD 2026 Financial Results

 

·YTD 2026 net income was $125.9 million or $2.07 per diluted share, an increase of 7.9% or $9.2 million and $0.11 per diluted share compared to YTD 2025 net income of $116.7 million or $1.96 per diluted share.

 

·YTD 2026 revenue increased $75.1 million or 9.6% to $855.3 million, compared to YTD revenue of $780.2 million in 2025.
◦Rate changes and new customers added $51.9 million in revenue.
◦DREMA added $3.9 million in revenue.
◦The Interim Rates Memorandum Account related to the recently completed 2024 California GRC proceeding added $15.7 million.
◦Recognition of remaining deferred revenue under California Water Service’s (Cal Water) previous Water Revenue Adjustment Mechanism (WRAM) added $9.9 million.
◦Decline in customer water usage offset revenue increases by $3.9 million.

 

·YTD 2026 operating expenses increased $48.2 million or 7.6% to $683.6 million compared to YTD operating expenses of $635.4 million in 2025.
◦Water production costs increased $14.1 million, primarily due to an increase in wholesale water rates, partially offset by lower customer usage.
◦Other operations expenses increased $19.9 million, primarily due to the recognition of $8.1 million of deferred WRAM costs and an increase of $5.5 million in water conservation program expenses.

 

 

 

 

◦Income tax expense on operating income increased $13.1 million due to higher pre-tax income and a scheduled reduction in the TCJA refund.

 

·Net other income decreased $9.5 million due to a $4.7 million decrease in the net periodic benefit credit, an increase of $4.1 million in business acquisition expenses, and a $3.0 million reduction in non-qualified benefit plan investment gains.

 

Investing in Infrastructure to Provide Safe, High-Quality Water Service

 

The Company continued to invest responsibly in infrastructure projects that enhance quality and reliability for customers, including water main replacements, groundwater wells, and water quality treatment projects. Group capital investments for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, were $424.8 million, up 16.5% compared to September 30, 2025, YTD investments of $364.7 million.

 

Working with Regulators to Recover Costs While Keeping Rates Affordable

 

In the third quarter, the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) granted Cal Water’s request to recover certain PFAS treatment costs using the advice letter process. In 2026, Cal Water has invested $23 million YTD in PFAS treatment projects and has 18 plants under construction. Company-wide, Group expects to invest $228.0 million in PFAS treatment and well replacement projects; to date, it has recovered $59.4 million in net proceeds from PFAS manufacturers to help reduce the rate impact on customers.

 

Importantly, Cal Water assesses customer affordability during every GRC. In 2025, median water bills were 0.88% of median household income, which is well within the affordability guideline of 2% established by the United States Environmental Protection Agency. In addition, Cal Water provides a low-income assistance program, an additional discount for customers in higher-cost service areas, customer conservation programs and rebates, and interest-free payment plans. It also actively pursues state and federal grants to help mitigate rate increases, especially in low-income communities.

 

In Washington, the WUTC adopted the joint settlement agreement of Washington Water, WUTC staff, and the Consumer Advocate team from the Public Counsel Unit of the Washington Attorney General’s Office, which authorizes rate increases that will increase annual revenues by $4.1 million, effective October 1, 2026.

 

After the close of the third quarter, the State of Hawaii Public Utilities Commission (HPUC) adopted a settlement agreement between Hawaii Water and the HPUC Consumer Advocate that authorizes rate increases in the Kapalua water and wastewater systems that will increase annual revenues by $1.94 million, effective October 21, 2026. This rate increase will be the first one implemented since these systems were acquired in 2021 and reflects the capital improvements Hawaii Water continues to make to improve water system reliability.

 

 

 

 

Progressing on Nevada and Oregon Acquisitions

 

On August 11, 2026, the Public Utilities Commission of Nevada approved California Water Service Group’s acquisition of Nexus Water Group’s (Nexus) Nevada systems and operations, and on October 27, 2026, the Oregon Public Utilities Commission (OPUC) followed suit. The Company expects to close the Nevada and Oregon acquisitions in the fourth quarter of 2026.

 

Maintaining a Strong Balance Sheet

 

As of September 30, 2026, Group’s cash and cash equivalents totaled $45.1 million and restricted cash was $45.8 million. Group and Cal Water had additional short-term borrowing capacity of $200.0 million and $225.0 million, respectively, subject to meeting the borrowing conditions on its Group and Cal Water lines of credit.

 

During the third quarter, the Company sold 1.0 million shares of common stock through its at-the-market equity program and raised proceeds of $49.8 million. In addition, the Company entered into forward sales agreements with investors to sell an additional 1.6 million shares. The Company has up to 12 months from date it entered into the forward contracts to settle the agreements. As of September 30, 2026, we could have physically settled with the investors for $77.8 million.

 

Subsequent to the end of the third quarter, the Company and Cal Water also entered into Note and First Mortgage Bond purchase agreements, respectively, which provide for the issuance of $125.0 million in unsecured borrowings (the Notes) and $150.0 million in secured borrowings (the Bonds). The Notes will accrue interest at 6.08% and 6.41% with maturities of December 15, 2031 and December 15, 2036. The Bonds will accrue interest at 6.49% and 6.54% with maturities of December 15, 2046 and December 15, 2056. The Notes and Bonds are expected to be issued on or before December 31, 2026.

 

With the long-term debt agreements and equity forward arrangements in place, the Company believes it has the resources to finance its on-going initiatives into the first half of 2027, including the acquisition of Nevada and Oregon systems which we expect to close before December 31, 2026.

 

The most recent Cost of Capital decision for Cal Water, which remains effective through December 2027, establishes a 10.27% authorized return on equity, a 7.46% authorized return on rate base, and a debt/equity structure of 46.6%/53.4%.

 

Increasing Dividends for 59 Consecutive Years

 

The Group Board of Directors has declared the 327th consecutive quarterly dividend in the amount of $0.335 per common share. The Company’s 59th consecutive annual dividend increase announced earlier this year is expected to yield an annual dividend of $1.34 per common share, an increase of 8.1% over 2025.

 

 

 

 

Preparing for Emergencies

 

Group continued to prioritize emergency preparedness in the third quarter, providing emergency preparedness and response training to employees and first responders; equipping and preparing its Operations Rapid Response Teams; positioning and fueling back-up power sources; testing satellite phones and two-way radios; maintaining vegetation to help minimize damage to key facilities in the event of storms or fires; and preparing to conduct additional water quality monitoring, if needed. These ongoing efforts are expected to better prepare the Company to deal with natural disasters, including wildfires and forecasted El Niño conditions.

 

Making an Impact in the Community

 

During the third quarter, Group celebrated its centennial with customers, awarded scholarships to students, and opened its firefighter grant program. The Company was named one of America’s Greatest Workplaces, America’s Greatest Companies, and World’s Greenest Companies by Newsweek. And Cal Water partnered with Selma Unified School District and Self-Help Enterprises to do the work necessary to connect Indianola Elementary School to its system in order to provide high-quality water to 400 students and faculty.

About California Water Service Group

 

Group is the parent company of regulated utilities California Water Service Company, Hawaii Water Service Company, Inc., New Mexico Water Service Company, and Washington Water Service Company, as well as TWSC, Inc. (Texas Water Service), a utility holding company. Together, these companies provide regulated and non-regulated water and wastewater service to more than 2.2 million people in California, Hawaii, New Mexico, Washington, and Texas. Group’s common stock trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol “CWT.” Additional information is available online at www.calwatergroup.com.
