Better Home & Finance Holding Company Announces Third Quarter 2026 Results

Better Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results, Provides Update on Strategic Direction

•Q3 2026 Loan Volume grew 15% year over year to $1.40 billion, within prior guidance range.
•Total Net Revenues grew 9% year over year to $45.1 million.
•Platform Loan Volume reached $792 million, representing 57% of Q3 2026 Loan Volume.
•Net Loss of $36.5 million, versus a $39.1 million Net Loss in Q3 2025, a 7% improvement year over year.
•Adjusted EBITDA loss of $29.7 million, compared to $22.9 million in Q3 2025, a 29% increase. Q3 2026 includes approximately $12.8 million of proxy-related legal, public relations, and other expenses as well as other one-time legal expenses. Excluding those expenses, Q3 2026 Adjusted EBITDA loss would have been approximately $16.8 million, within the previously issued guidance range.

NEW YORK -- (BUSINESS WIRE) -- November 9, 2026 -- Better Home & Finance Holding Company (NASDAQ: BETR; BETRW), the AI-native mortgage and home equity finance company, today announced results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

“I’m excited to be back and executing on our vision for Better 2.0,” said Vishal Garg, Founder and Head of Product, Platform and Innovation at Better. "From 2019 to 2021, Better was one of the fastest-growing companies in mortgage because we moved faster and let our AI-native platform do the work. During my absence, under Mr. Lewis and the prior Board, we lacked that edge. Loan officer talk time fell 29%, production hours fell 25%, and key partnership launches stalled, weighing on results. Better 2.0 will follow our playbook that works: fewer layers, more AI, every number in public.”

“When I came back we assessed the current rate environment and under this challenging backdrop our priorities became clear: stronger execution, higher productivity, and lower costs,” Garg continued.



“Since my return, we have executed $3.5 million in annualized savings from delayering our management and corporate structure, with AI agents and dashboards. We intend to scale without rebuilding those management layers.

Early results are encouraging. Following the introduction of AI lead routing, weekend loan locks doubled from October 24th to the 31st. Our focus is on turning these improvements into more funded loans, lower costs, and sustainable profitability,” Garg added.

Third Quarter 2026 Financial Highlights

Following the classification of Birmingham Bank as held for sale and discontinued operations in the first quarter of 2026, prior-period results have been reclassified to conform to the current-period presentation.

GAAP Results

•Total Net Revenues of $45.1 million, compared to $41.5 million in Q3 2025, up 9% YoY.
•Net Loss of $36.5 million, compared to $39.1 million in Q3 2025, a 7% YoY improvement.

Key Operating Metrics and Non-GAAP Financial Measures

•Adjusted EBITDA loss of $29.7 million, versus a $22.9 million loss in Q3 2025.
•Loan Volume of $1.40 billion, compared to $1.21 billion in Q3 2025, demonstrating 15% YoY growth.
•4,748 Total Loans, compared to 4,086 in Q3 2025, demonstrating 16% YoY growth.

•By product:
◦Refinance Loan Volume of $356 million (25.5% of Q3 2026 Loan Volume)
◦Purchase Loan Volume of $776 million (55.6% of Q3 2026 Loan Volume)
◦HELOC Loan Volume of $263 million (18.9% of Q3 2026 Loan Volume)
•By channel:
◦Platform Loan Volume of $792 million (57% of Q3 2026 Loan Volume)
◦D2C Loan Volume of $603 million (43% of Q3 2026 Loan Volume)
•Ended Q3 2026 with $77.5 million of cash and cash equivalents and $12.9 million of restricted cash.

"We delivered loan volume within our guidance range despite a challenging rate environment, with platform channels contributing 57% of volume and HELOCs representing 19%,” said Loveen Advani, Chief Financial Officer of Better.

"With the proxy contest behind us, we have renewed our focus on profitable growth and operating efficiency. We are reducing fixed costs and using AI to improve productivity, with a goal of scaling volume without rebuilding overhead. Reaching sustainable Adjusted EBITDA breakeven remains our top financial priority.” Advani continued.

Guidance:

Q4 2026 Adjusted EBITDA-basis Operating Expenses: $45.0 million to $47.0 million



A reconciliation of Adjusted EBITDA-basis Operating Expenses to Net Loss-basis Operating Expenses on a forward-looking basis cannot be provided without unreasonable efforts, as the Company is unable to provide reconciling information with respect to benefit for income taxes, stock-based compensation, changes in fair value of warrant liabilities, and goodwill impairment, all of which are adjustments to Adjusted EBITDA.

Third Quarter 2026 Operational Highlights

•Loan Volume grew 15% year over year to $1.40 billion, compared to $1.21 billion in Q3 2025.
•Platform Loan Volume grew 64% year over year to $792 million, representing a record 57% of Loan Volume, up from 55% in Q2 2026 and 50% in Q1 2026.
•HELOC Loan Volume totaled $263 million, representing 19% of Loan Volume, up from 18% in Q2 2026 and 12% in Q1 2026.

Subsequent Events in Q4 2026

•A group led by Vishal Garg secured written consents from stockholders representing 52% of the Company’s outstanding voting power, resulting in the removal of five directors from the Board as well as the Interim CEO.
•Launched Better’s AI-powered HELOC with a large existing partner, expanding access to its established customer base without upfront customer acquisition costs.
•Expanded the wholesale platform to 39 signed brokerages, initially focused on HELOCs to deliver a seamless experience for brokers and borrowers.
•Completed actions expected to deliver $30.4 million in annualized savings toward a $64.2 million target, including reductions in management layers, greater use of AI, and removing vendor spend.

About Better

Better Home & Finance Holding Company (NASDAQ: BETR) is the first AI-native mortgage and home equity finance platform, and first fintech to fund more than $110 billion in loan volume. Since 2016, Better has leveraged its industry-leading AI platform, Tinman®, to achieve a singular mission of making homeownership cheaper, faster, and easier for all Americans. Tinman® allows customers to see their rate options in seconds, get pre-approved in minutes, lock in rates, and close their loan in as little as three weeks. In addition, Betsy®, leveraging Tinman® MCP, the first AI loan agent built exclusively for the mortgage industry, is revolutionizing the homebuying journey by delivering timely application status updates to consumers, answering questions, and moving their loan application along 24/7/365. Better’s


mortgage offerings include GSE-conforming, FHA, VA, jumbo, and Non-QM mortgage loans as well as home equity loans. Better serves customers in all 50 US states.

Selected Financial Data, Non-GAAP Measures and Definitions

The following tables present selected financial data, reconciliations of non-GAAP financial measures to the most directly comparable GAAP measures, and definitions of key metrics.

Key Metrics

•Funded Loan Volume: the principal amount of loans funded in a period.
•Loan Volume: Funded Loan Volume plus Processed Volume.
•Processed Volume: loans processed on the Tinman platform for strategic partners but not funded by Better.
•Purchase Loan Volume and Refinance Loan Volume: the principal amount of purchase or refinance loans funded in a period.
•HELOC Loan Volume: the principal amount of HELOC and closed-end second-lien loans funded in a period.
•D2C Loan Volume: loans from direct customer interactions through all channels other than partners and the Tinman channel.
•Platform Loan Volume: loans funded through Tinman partner relationships.


•Total Loans: the number of purchase, refinance, HELOC, and closed-end second-lien loans, including Processed loans.

Use of Non-GAAP Financial Measures

We include certain financial measures not presented in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP”) including Adjusted EBITDA.

We calculate Adjusted EBITDA as net income (loss) adjusted for the impact of stock-based compensation expense, change in the fair value of warrants, equity-related liabilities, transaction expenses, loss from discontinued operations, and other non-recurring or non-core operational expenses, as well as interest and amortization on non-funding debt (which includes interest on the Convertible Note (as defined in our Form 10-K), depreciation and amortization expense, and income tax (benefit)/expense).

This non-GAAP financial measure should not be considered in isolation and is not intended to be a substitute for any GAAP financial measure. This non-GAAP measure provides supplemental information that we believe helps investors better understand our business, our business model and how we analyze our performance. We also believe this non-GAAP financial measure improves investors’ and analysts’ ability to compare our results with those of our competitors and other similarly situated companies, which commonly disclose similar performance measures.

However, our calculation of Adjusted EBITDA may not be comparable to similarly titled performance measures presented by other companies. Further, although we use this non-GAAP measure to assess the financial performance of our business, this measure excludes certain substantial costs related to our business, and investors are cautioned not to use such measures as a substitute for financial results prepared according to GAAP. Non-GAAP financial measures have limitations in their usefulness to investors because they have no standardized meaning prescribed by GAAP and are not prepared under any comprehensive set of accounting rules or principles. As a result, non-GAAP financial measures should be viewed as supplementing, and not as an alternative or substitute for, our financial results prepared and presented in accordance with GAAP.

(2)Stock-based compensation represents the non-cash grant date fair value of stock-based instruments utilized to incentivize employees and consultants recognized over the applicable vesting period. This expense is a non-cash expense. (6)Transaction expenses consist of legal fees incurred in connection with the sale of London House Exchange Ltd., the proposed sale of Birmingham Bank, and a bridge financing arrangement.
