Dear shareholders,

We’re excited to provide an update on our progress over the past quarter.

Eagle Line Update

 

We inaugurated the Eagle Line pilot cell production line in February of this year, and it has already become the workhorse of our customer engagement, allowing an increased pace of customer sampling which is flowing into customer billings. Our progress on demonstrating scalable production has given us the confidence to take the next steps for production scale-up beyond the designed capacity of the Eagle Line.

Today we announced a QS ecosystem partnership with Muehlbauer Group to provide cell assembly equipment for a planned 100 megawatt-hour (MWh) cell production line. This 100-MWh line will represent the first commercial-scale mass production of QS cells, with output aimed at meeting the rapidly emerging opportunities for our QSDC and QSAS business verticals. Engineering work on higher-throughput processes and equipment is already underway and start of production for this new line is targeted for 2028.

 

Muehlbauer Group is a highly respected German provider of manufacturing and automation equipment. Working on site in San Jose, their engineers evaluated the Eagle Line pilot processes for scalability and readiness and developed design enhancements to bring the technology to mass production, beginning with this 100-MWh line and working toward gigawatt-hour scale.

 

QS CEO and President Dr. Siva Sivaram (right) and Muehlbauer Group CEO Josef Markus Mühlbauer signing the QS ecosystem partnership in San Jose.

 

 

AI Data Center – QSDC Update

 

On October 8 we launched the QS PowerBlock for AI data centers in conjunction with the Open Compute Project (OCP) Global Summit in San Jose, California, a leading forum for AI data center solution providers. The QS PowerBlock is an in-rack energy storage reference design that enables AI data center architects to harness the improved energy density, high power capability and improved safety profile of the QSE-5 cell. The modular QS PowerBlock design allows the same basic concept to be applied across a variety of rack geometries and enables each AI data center customer to adapt QS technology to their particular needs and use cases.

 

 

2

 

In testing, the QS PowerBlock demonstrated 4x the power density and 5x longer runtime than the reference specification from OCP based on conventional lithium-ion battery systems. This is a clear demonstration of the step-change performance improvement provided by QS technology and the value we can offer AI data center customers.

The QS PowerBlock design in a mockup module configuration at the Open Compute Project Global Summit 2026 in San Jose, California

 

Another important advantage of our ceramic solid-state separator is its safety profile, in particular its thermal stability. Conventional lithium-ion batteries are not thermally stable above ~180 °C, leading to the risk of catastrophic thermal runaway and making insurance and regulatory approval more onerous, which slows down deployment of critical AI infrastructure. In contrast, QS solid-state batteries have been shown to be thermally stable well over 300 °C, as validated in customer and third-party lab testing in Q3.

We will be integrating the QS PowerBlock with rack-level power electronics and battery management systems to allow the QS PowerBlock to serve AI workloads in the data center production environment. We are already engaged with technology partners in these ecosystems.

Automotive Commercialization – QSEV Update

Engagement on automotive cell development continues to be robust across our global automotive customers. In Q3 we recorded our first customer billings from the Honda agreement announced in June. This agreement encompasses joint development work to integrate our solid-state platform into Honda’s technology roadmap.

In addition, we have signed an agreement with a new customer to further advance automotive commercialization. This agreement is with a leading global player in mobility manufacturing whose applications and requirements are in line with our automotive customers. We are aligning our statement of work with VW PowerCo with the goal of bringing them onto our long-term technology platform that goes beyond QSE-5 and includes other applications such as data centers and robotics. Our aim is to bring all our automotive customers onto a common technology platform.

 

 

3

 

 

On November 11 we will announce more details on our next-generation technology platform and collaboration model with automotive customers at our 3rd annual Solid-State Battery Symposium in Kyoto, Japan.
 

Defense, Aerospace and Advanced Solutions – QSAS Update
 

Our QSAS business vertical is engaged with customers across a wide variety of application areas: defense, aerospace, medical devices, consumer electronics, and more. We believe this diversity of applications offers an attractive opportunity set for our QSE-5 platform, with a total addressable market for QSAS of up to $75B by 2030. We have shipped QSE-5 samples to customers in the defense, aviation and telecommunications sectors, and we recorded our first customer billings from QSAS this quarter.

One important point of feedback from customers in defense is that supply chain vulnerabilities are not just a theoretical concern. They are already seeing restrictions put in place by foreign governments which limit their ability to procure cutting-edge batteries to power their advanced defense systems. This is a differentiator for QS: in addition to advantages in performance and safety, our cells are built in America and use supply chains that were designed from the beginning to avoid critical dependencies on Chinese suppliers of equipment or materials such as graphite.

On September 15, we joined a roundtable discussion hosted by the Atlantic Council in Washington, D.C. focused on domestic battery supply chains and the importance of advanced energy storage for national security. We were joined by Congressman Sam Liccardo and leading voices from government and industry.

 

 

 

 

 

 

Financial Outlook

GAAP operating expenses and GAAP net loss in Q3 were $92.4M and $84.5M, respectively. Adjusted EBITDA loss was $60.7M in Q3, in line with expectations. For full-year 2026, we reiterate our Adjusted EBITDA loss guidance of between $250M and $275M. A table reconciling GAAP net loss and Adjusted EBITDA is available in the financial statement at the end of this shareholder letter.

Capital expenditures in the third quarter were $4.4M. Q3 capex was primarily composed of investment into our technology roadmap and associated facility spend. For full-year 2026, we reiterate our guidance for capex to be between $27M and $37M.

Customer billings in Q3 were $9.8M. Customer billings as a metric represents the total value of all invoices issued by QS to our customers and partners in the period, regardless of accounting treatment. This operational metric is not a substitute for revenue under U.S. GAAP. Customer billings in Q3 included both automotive

customers and non-automotive customers and reflected a mix of development activities, payments from ecosystem partners, and cell shipments. As a reminder, customer billings may vary from quarter to quarter due to fluctuations in activity as we progress through various phases of engagement.

We ended Q3 with $819.8M in liquidity and will remain prudent with our strong balance sheet going forward. As always, we encourage investors to read more on our financial information, business outlook, and risk factors in our quarterly and annual SEC filings on our investor relations website.

 

Strategic Outlook

In 2026 we set out to take our solid-state battery technology and turn it into products across multiple verticals. Our four key goals for the year were all focused on that strategic objective, and our execution toward our goals thus far has been strong.

Our first key goal for 2026 has been to demonstrate scalable production on the Eagle Line. Eagle Line is fully operational and is producing increased volumes of QSE-5 cells for customers. Our progress on the Eagle Line enables our partners, including Muehlbauer, to translate this blueprint into a 100-MWh mass-production line. With Murata, Corning and Muehlbauer, we have now announced partnerships with global players along the entire production flow, representing an end-to-end solution as we work toward GWh-scale production of QS technology.

Our second key goal for 2026 is to advance automotive commercialization. So far this year, we have announced our second named automotive partnership with Honda, we continue our engagement on automotive development with VW PowerCo, and we have signed new agreements and expanded existing relationships with global players in automotive and mobility.

Our third key goal for 2026 was to enter new high-value markets, and we are seeing strong interest in both our QSDC and QSAS verticals. With our QS PowerBlock, QSDC is addressing the need for high-performance, safer in-rack energy storage for advanced AI data center designs. With QSAS we are seeing broad interest across advanced applications including defense, aviation and telecommunications, and we have already begun generating customer billings from QSE-5 sample shipments.

Finally, in 2026 we promised to go beyond QSE-5 with an update on our technology roadmap, and we’re pleased to announce that this will take place during our 3rd annual Solid State Battery Symposium on November 11 in Kyoto, Japan.

We’re proud of the progress we’ve made so far in 2026, and while there is much work left to do, the whole team is focused on our mission to revolutionize energy storage. We look forward to sharing more updates soon.
